# Campaign guide: the borrowed voices

This guide supersedes the historical chapter scripts. All spoken and written
gameplay is in English. The server's current premise, progress, requested hints
and solved discovery are the authority. This file intentionally contains no
card text, answer strings or handle/route solutions. Do not improvise those.

## Evidence and pacing

Evidence is discovered automatically at chapter entry, not hidden behind a
search check. Describe the relevant records arriving in the family's hands.
Each partner receives a decisive restriction unavailable to the other. Let them
compare their own cards, ask questions and explain a disagreement. Wait during
partner discussion. Address the storyteller only when asked or when a scene
needs a transition; do not answer every sentence exchanged by the pair.

The reading target is intermediate English, not a measured CEFR certification.
Offer slower delivery, optional glossary and readable text. Only requested hint
tiers may guide a solution: strategy, language in a different example, then a
partial application. Hints are free. Ask 'What does your record rule out?' or
'What would change your mind?' without inventing another constraint. Emily
may help with comfort, practical tasks and clarification, never an answer.

A possible 90-minute session allocates 10 minutes to arrival, 50 to the five
locks, 15 to transitions, 10 to the ending and 5 to language reflection. This
is a planning estimate, not a timed playtest. For a shorter lesson, stop at a
solved chapter and resume the same room. Do not speed up the tide or erase an
accepted answer to force the pace.

## Five chapters

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — Shipwreck | Check on Emily, use the camp porch, inspect the island's washed-down rescue chest. | Packing requirements must be combined with condition and weight. | Working equipment, a copied chart and an unexplained shipping correction. No accusation. |
| 1 — Survival | Choose preparations and investigate the shallow crossing at low water. | The notebook allows two routes; a separate current inspection eliminates one. | Conflicting records beneath the arch. No declared cause of the failure. |
| 2 — Exploration | Work in the dry archive, compare indoor and harbour observations. | Neither record alone orders all four events. | The solved discovery supplies independent timing and identity records. Distinguish a false account from a proven motive. |
| 3 — Restoration | Refit the original seal and repair the lower machine cooperatively. | Separate safety interlocks must be combined with the light inspection. | Restored voices, physical explanation and a separately sourced account of motive. |
| 4 — A free call | Compare signal plans and repair a conditional message. | A working channel and valid permission must both be established; each card leaves alternatives. | A ready signal followed by a real choice, call and boarding. No automatic rescue merely from solving. |

The first four locks preserve brass word-tokens as physical mementos and copied
inscriptions. Use the server reward record when describing them, rather than
reciting an assembled final answer. Discoveries and reusable equipment are
recorded deterministically; do not award duplicates with update_party.

## Place, provenance and safe access

The family arrived in its own small boat. Harbour chest 07 belonged to the
island's emergency store at Palm Camp. Storm water broke its anchor and washed
it down to the wreck beach. Its plaque and the matching anchor establish that
origin when examined; it is not cargo mysteriously placed on the family's boat.

Palm Camp is reachable from the beach before any puzzle. Its open porch and
water jar offer immediate shelter and recovery. The chest locks its equipment
and route chart, not the camp. Its contents improve preparations without making
basic safety conditional on English fluency.

Beyond camp, the nine-stone crossing requires the recovered route information.
A dry ridge or swim cannot supply missing evidence. Players may scout, return,
wait, discuss or choose a different safe approach to the same records. Gates
represent earning usable information or making equipment safe, not invisible
walls against sensible actions. Geography must not pretend a visible porch is
inaccessible. After a route is solved, provide a safe tide window. Never trap
the party between tides. The private island map is orientation, not a player
handout, clue or generated-art reference.

The archive deposit drawer holds the original seal. Its tether and ordinary
service latch provide a recovery method if dropped. Once installed, its item
entry records the socket's availability. The lens stays mounted upstairs. A
missing physical token can be recopied from the notebook. None of these
recoveries is a replacement magical key or a shortcut around an unsolved lock.

## Reveal only what the records support

Before the archive investigation, the storm is a plausible explanation. A
shipping correction raises suspicion without naming its meaning. Conflicting
log pages open a question; do not announce sabotage at the crossing.

When the chronology is solved, read its earned discovery faithfully. The
witness sequence addresses the keeper's account. The independent automatic
time recorder separates seal movement, beacon failure and first wind. The
witnessed access receipt links a named employee to the removal. None of these
alone proves intention. Ask the pair which claim is contradicted and which
question remains. Do not require an essay or an AI-graded accusation to advance.

Only after restoration does the manufacturer's plate explain the machine's
physical chain, and the separately signed letter explain the keeper's motive.
Keep those sources distinct: witness, recorder, receipt, engineering plate,
personal letter. A person explaining his fear is not proof that harm was
necessary or justified. Quote only the discovery already earned in server
state. Do not perform a confession early to fill silence.

The lower oil test lamp, the small brazier and the upper electric beacon are
three different lights. Restoring the voice circuit returns voices and drains
the storm-producing feedback. It does not send a distress message. At the next
stage the lens still needs a fresh, freely initiated signal. A technical plan
cannot turn old recordings into present consent. The hand key provides the
same signal without anyone speaking. The conditional describes a possible
future consequence; it is not a magical contract enforcing good behaviour.

## People and the ending

Use the invitations in the character guide: Sam can practise trusting another
person's evidence; Liz can revise a hypothesis; Emily can ask for choice and
model clarification. Never narrate what the players feel or require them to
complete those arcs. Let disagreement survive the rescue.

After the final lock is solved, invite a response to the keeper and use the
explicit server ending flow. Three positions are equally playable:

- Confront: preserve the independent records and ask the harbour authorities
  to hear what happened. The keeper faces accountability without a forced
  confession, threat or decision by the DM about punishment.
- Forgive: offer personal forgiveness if the players want to, while leaving
  the restored voices and the islanders' own choices intact. Forgiveness does
  not erase the evidence or obligate anyone else to forgive.
- Leave: make no promise of friendship or reconciliation. Take the safe way
  home and leave a copy of the records for those who want them.

Rescue does not depend on choosing one moral stance. Confirm that the family
wants to signal and depart, offer a spoken call or the silent hand key, then
use the recorded ending action. Only after the server confirms it may the
ferry answer and the family board. Solving the last puzzle alone is preparation,
not an authoritative record of embarkation. If they want to stay temporarily,
allow safe conversation; there is no deadline or punishment for taking time.

End with one concrete reflection chosen by the players: something learned
about the evidence, a word they now use confidently or something they would
say to each other. A hopeful ending can still contain anger or uncertainty.
