# The family

| Character | STR | INT | SUR | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Samuel (Sam) | 5 | 2 | 3 | Player: outdoor guide and former military officer |
| Elizabeth (Liz) | 2 | 5 | 3 | Player: archaeologist and field researcher |
| Emily | 2 | 3 | 5 | NPC: their sixteen-year-old daughter |

## Sam

Sam is resourceful and protective. He survived harsh conditions before, but
this island asks for something he cannot supply alone: another person's
information. Early on, invite him to ask Liz what she needs before proposing a
plan. After a shared success, acknowledge one specific contribution he trusted.
At the end, invite him to say what safety means when other people remain free
to leave. These are invitations, never prescribed emotions or graded answers.
He starts with a pocket knife; supplies come from the solved harbour chest.

## Liz

Liz is curious and good at constructing explanations from fragments. Offer an
early chance to propose a tentative explanation for the dark lighthouse. When
the records disagree, ask what evidence would change her mind. Let her revise,
reserve judgment or explain an uncertainty without losing face. At the end,
she may preserve the records for accountability, offer them to the islanders or
leave a copy. None of those choices destroys permanent campaign knowledge.
She starts with a field notebook, which preserves discoveries and inscriptions.

## Emily

Emily is brave, observant and experienced outdoors. Give her useful actions:
check the camp's water jar, lay out dry cloth, hold a light or ask someone to
repeat a word. She can model asking for clarification without knowing an answer.
She has no secret third card, supernatural intuition or authority to grade a
solution. Do not let her translate both records into an answer for the adults.

After the motive is discovered, Emily may ask, 'Did anyone get to choose?'
Allow the players to answer or disagree. At departure she can choose whether to
speak her own farewell, remain silent or tap the hand key if the family invites
her. Never force the players' voices or emotional responses through her.
